# How should future value be measured?

# Future Value Definition

## Purpose

The primary objective of Athlete Career Intelligence is to identify basketball players whose future professional value exceeds current market expectations.

To achieve this, the platform requires a measurable definition of **Future Value**.

Future Value represents the expected value generated by a player's professional career after leaving college.

Rather than focusing on a single statistic, Future Value is intended to capture a broader view of career success, including performance, longevity, impact, and market relevance.

---

# Why Future Value Matters

Sports agencies operate under uncertainty.

At any given moment, agents must decide which players deserve their attention, resources, and long-term investment.

The challenge is that current performance does not always translate into future success.

Some players dominate at the college level but struggle professionally.

Others enter professional basketball with limited expectations and eventually become valuable NBA contributors.

The purpose of Future Value is to provide a consistent framework for evaluating long-term player potential.

---

# Design Principles

The Future Value Score should be:

### Multi-Dimensional

A player's value cannot be explained by a single metric.

The score should incorporate multiple indicators of career success.

### Long-Term Oriented

The objective is not to predict next-season performance.

The objective is to estimate long-term career outcomes.

### Position-Agnostic

The framework should allow comparisons across different player profiles and positions.

### Explainable

The score should be understandable by scouts, agents, analysts, and decision-makers.

### Expandable

The methodology should support new metrics and data sources as the project evolves.

---

# What Does Success Look Like?

A successful player typically demonstrates one or more of the following:

- Long professional career
- Consistent NBA contribution
- Strong advanced impact metrics
- Recognition and awards
- Financial success
- Ability to remain valuable across multiple seasons

Future Value should attempt to measure these dimensions collectively.

---

# Candidate Components

The first version of the Future Value Score may include the following factors:

## Career Longevity

Measures how long a player remains in the league.

Potential metrics:

- NBA Seasons Played
- Career Games Played
- Career Minutes Played

---

## Career Impact

Measures the player's on-court contribution.

Potential metrics:

- Career Win Shares
- Career VORP
- BPM
- Advanced Impact Metrics

---

## Career Recognition

Measures achievements and league recognition.

Potential metrics:

- All-Star Selections
- All-NBA Teams
- Awards
- Championships

---

## Market Value

Measures the player's economic value.

Potential metrics:

- Career Earnings
- Contract Value
- Guaranteed Salaries

*Note: Market value information may be incorporated in future project phases.*

---

# Future Value Score (Conceptual)

The first version of the platform will combine several career outcomes into a single score ranging from 0 to 100.

Conceptually:

```text
Future Value Score =
Career Longevity
+ Career Impact
+ Career Recognition
+ Market Value
```

The precise weighting methodology will be determined during the modelling phase.

---

# Initial MVP Definition

For the first project iteration, Future Value will prioritize variables that are:

- Publicly available
- Historically consistent
- Easy to validate

Therefore, the MVP will likely focus on:

```text
Future Value Score =
40% Career Win Shares
+ 30% NBA Seasons Played
+ 20% Career Minutes Played
+ 10% Awards & Recognition
```

This weighting is provisional and subject to experimentation.

# Future Questions

The following questions remain open and will be explored during development:

## Value vs Potential

Should the model measure:

- Actual career value?
- Expected future value?
- Both?

---

## Context Effects

How much of a player's success depends on:

- Team environment
- Coaching
- System fit
- Teammates

Can context be measured?

---

## Hidden Value

Can the model identify players who were undervalued by the draft process but later became successful professionals?

Examples:

- Jalen Brunson
- Draymond Green
- Fred VanVleet
- Nikola Jokic

---

# Long-Term Vision

The ultimate objective is not to predict statistics.

The goal is to build a framework capable of answering a much more important question:

> If a sports agency could only invest in a limited number of players today, which players are most likely to create the highest future value over the course of their careers?

Every model, dashboard, similarity engine, scouting report, and career simulator developed within Athlete Career Intelligence will be designed to help answer that question.
